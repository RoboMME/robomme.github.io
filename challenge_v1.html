Challenge Overview 🚀

The RoboMME Challenge evaluates how well memory-augmented robotic generalist policies can handle long-horizon, history-dependent manipulation tasks. It is hosted as part of the Foundation Models Meet Embodied Agents (FMEA) Workshop @ CVPR 2026 .

Teams will be ranked on the RoboMME Challenge leaderboard hosted on EvalAI, and top entries will be highlighted live during the workshop session. 🏅 The 1st, 2nd, and 3rd place winners will receive prizes of $300, $200, and $100, respectively.

What You Do 🧪

  1. 🔁 Train your models on the released RoboMME training data.
  2. 📊 Evaluate your models using the RoboMME benchmark tools on the open-source test episodes .
  3. 🌐 Serve your policy as a server by following the submission instructions. We will remotely evaluate your policy on our held-out test set.

Evaluation 🎯

Phase 1 — Partial Evaluation. 🔍 We run an initial evaluation on a small subset of the open-source test episodes for all participants. This phase checks that each policy server is accessible and stable, and that performance is within a reasonable range. We then select the top 5–10 participants to move on.

Phase 2 — Full Evaluation. 🧮 We evaluate the selected participants on the full held-out test set. The final ranking is determined by performance on this full test set.

FAQ 📚

Q: What's the difference between the RoboMME Challenge leaderboard and the regular RoboMME leaderboard?

A: The regular leaderboard is evaluated by participants themselves, who run the evaluation and open a pull request to update their results. Submissions can be made at any time. The RoboMME Challenge leaderboard is evaluated by the organizers using a set of held-out test episodes, and the results are updated and maintained on EvalAI. Submissions are only accepted during the challenge period.

Q: Can I use external data or other VLA backbones?

A: Yes. Any methods or resources are allowed, but you may not use the RoboMME repository itself to generate additional training data, as this would be unfair. You are welcome to use training data beyond the RoboMME repository, as long as all external data and resources are clearly described in your method description.

Q: Can I use human-in-the-loop methods during testing?

A: No. Participants must not attempt to manually intervene policy rollouts, as this would unfairly influence the evaluation results.

Q: Can I write rules or design prompts to improve policy performance?

A: The goal of RoboMME is to evaluate robotic generalist policies, so we do not encourage participants to write task-specific rules or prompts solely to boost performance on particular tasks.

Q: Is there a team size limit?

A: There is no strict limit, but each team should submit under a single team name and submit only one model.

Q: Will top teams need to provide extra details?

A: Yes. Top teams will be asked to share a brief method description and reproducibility details.

Q: Where can I find baselines and the starter kit?

Baselines, environment setup, and evaluation scripts are available in the official RoboMME repository and the RoboMME policy learning repository. You can start by downloading MME-VLA checkpoints from Hugging Face and evaluate them locally using the code in the repository.
